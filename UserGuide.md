# Beginner Guide

<a id="what-this-app-is-about"></a>

## What This App Is About

Meditation & Breathwork helps you stay present while gently observing how your body responds during meditation and breathwork.

It allows you to:

- run simple or structured sessions
- receive vibration cues instead of visual distractions
- observe heart rate, HRV, stress, and breathing
- save sessions to Garmin Connect to notice patterns over time

The goal is **awareness, not performance**.

If you would like a short, motivating reflection on why slowing down matters (and why it is so hard in a fast-paced culture), **[Cory Muscara's The Practicing Human podcast - “Why You Need to Slow Down”](https://practicinghuman.buzzsprout.com/597910/episodes/18537284-why-you-need-to-slow-down)** explores how speed can erode relationships, happiness, and clear decision-making.

<a id="starting-your-first-session"></a>

## Starting Your First Session

1. Open the app.
2. Select a session from the list.
3. Press **Start** (or tap the screen on touch devices).
4. Try to keep your wrist still for the first moments - this helps sensors settle.

During the session you will see:

- elapsed time
- current heart rate
- HRV status
- stress and respiration (if supported)
- small colored markers showing upcoming vibration alerts

You can:

- pause or resume with the **back button**
- stop the session with the **start/stop button**
- control screen brightness via your watch buttons or touch
- press **up** or **down** (or swipe) for the **zen screen**: a dark screen with only the progress
  ring. The time shows for a few seconds after you switch, then fades. The session records exactly
  the same either way.

Your next session opens on the screen you ended this one on.

When you stop, you can save or discard the session.  
Pressing back on that prompt saves the session.  
Saved sessions appear in Garmin Connect after syncing.

<a id="a-simple-way-to-meditate"></a>

## A Simple Way to Meditate

If you do not know how to meditate, you are not alone - and you are not behind.  
You are already doing the most important thing: showing up.

Meditation is not about stopping thoughts, emptying the mind, or doing anything “right”. Even very experienced meditators notice their thoughts drifting every few seconds. That is normal.

The practice is not staying focused.  
The practice is noticing when you are not - and gently coming back.

If, for your first year of meditation, you did nothing more than what is described below, that would already be enough. Truly.

And one more thing: the more **humor, gentleness, and loving-kindness** you can bring to your sessions, the more enjoyable the whole experience becomes - and often, the more helpful it is. You are not trying to “win” meditation. You are learning how to be with yourself.

---

### A Very Simple Practice

You do not need to visualize anything or breathe in a special way.

1. Sit or lie down in a way that feels okay.
2. Let your breath happen naturally.
3. Bring your attention to the feeling of the breath at your nose.

You might notice:

- cool air as you inhale
- warmer air as you exhale
- subtle movement or tingling

That is enough. Nothing else to achieve.

If it helps, you can quietly label it: _“in”_ … _“out”_.  
Soft and simple.

---

### When the Mind Wanders - It Will!

At some point - often after just a few seconds - your mind will wander.

You might start thinking about:

- what you should be doing
- something from the past
- something that annoys you
- absolutely nothing important at all

This does not mean you are bad at meditation. It simply means your mind is doing what minds do.

Sometimes the mind even tries to pull you back into activity, tension, or a bit of chaos - because that is what feels familiar and safe. The nervous system can cling to a kind of familiar hell before it learns to trust an unfamiliar heaven. And these thoughts may not sound random at all - they can sound very convincing, like intuition, or even like this is simply who you are. It may offer you “very urgent” thoughts like:  
_“We should definitely reorganize our life right now.”_

You can meet that moment with a smile and hold it with love:

> “Ah, there you are again. You miss the chaos. Sneaky mind. I love you.”

---

### The Most Important Part

The moment you notice that your attention has drifted is not a failure. It is the moment the practice is actually happening.

When you notice:

- pause for a second
- maybe even smile
- gently and kindly ask yourself:
  _“Do I want to come back to the breath right now?”_

If the answer is yes, return to the feeling of the breath at the nose.  
If the answer is no, that is okay too - you can ask again later.

Every return is a small training moment.  
And every time you notice, you can quietly celebrate it: _“Oh - I noticed.”_ That is awareness waking up.

---

### When Something Feels Uncomfortable

Sometimes it is not a thought that pulls you away, but a feeling: restlessness, boredom, an itch, sadness, a tight chest.

You do not have to fix it or get rid of it. See if you can stay with it for a few breaths, just noticing what it feels like, then come back to the breath. Each time you stay a little, without reacting, you are learning that you can feel something unpleasant and still be okay.

If it becomes too much, you can always open your eyes, move, or end the session. That is part of practicing kindly, not a failure.

---

### Be Kind With Yourself

Coming back again and again is not something to be annoyed about. It is the training.

Each time you notice and return:

- you strengthen awareness
- you build patience
- you teach your nervous system that calm is safe

So if you catch yourself being harsh, see if you can soften it just a little:  
_“Of course my mind wandered. That is what minds do. Back we go.”_

Kindness works better than force. Curiosity works better than discipline.  
And humor makes the whole thing more human.

---

### If You Want to Go Deeper

This simple practice can take you very far on its own.

If, at some point, you feel curious to learn more about meditation - especially about working with restlessness, fear, and kindness toward yourself - a wonderful place to start is:

**Pema Chödrön - _How to Meditate: A Practical Guide to Making Friends with Your Mind_** (Sounds True, 2013).

And if you are moving through change, letting go, or feeling that part of practice is learning how to say goodbye to an old way of being, this podcast episode - **[Brave Souls - “Kissing your old life goodbye”](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0lbaZMdJm7E90vmqYTTs0I)** - is a beautiful companion. If you do not have much time - or want to jump to a part that may really move something in you - start at around 27 minutes and stay with it until the end, ideally with earphones on.

There is no rush. These are here whenever curiosity shows up.

---

### That is Really It

There is nothing else you need to do.

If your mind wanders 100 times, and you come back 100 times - that was a good session.  
If you stayed with the breath for only a few seconds at a time - that was a good session.

Meditation is not about staying calm. It is about learning how to return.

And you are allowed to do that gently - with warmth, humor, and a little self-respect.

<a id="choosing-a-session"></a>

## Choosing a Session

From the session picker you can scroll through available sessions and see:

- session duration
- vibration pattern
- interval alert timing
- HRV indicator

The app learns from the sessions you save which session you do at which time of day, on weekdays
and at the weekend. It opens where you left it; if you did not touch it since your last session,
it opens on the session you usually do around now. Being up to about three hours late or early
still counts. A session you do once does not change the suggestion; doing a new one twice in a row
does. If you reopen the app within an hour after a session, it opens
on the session you usually do next. Discarded sessions are not learned. To turn this off, see
[Learn Routine](#global-settings-defaults-for-all-sessions).

### HRV Indicator

- **Off** - HRV tracking disabled
- **HRV starting / Please wait** - the sensor is still coming online
- **Restart the app** - shown if HRV has not started after about 20 seconds; see [HRV does not start](#faq-hrv) for why this happens and how to fix it
- **HRV weak** - heartbeat data is arriving intermittently; hold still for a moment
- **Ready** - HRV tracking active  
  For reliable HRV data, minimize wrist movement.

<a id="breathwork-sessions"></a>

## Breathwork Sessions

The app includes preconfigured breathwork sessions that guide you breath by breath.  
A breathing cycle can include:

**Inhale → Hold → Exhale → Hold**

(Some techniques skip certain parts.)

> **New to breathwork?** Do not start here. A watch can keep your rhythm, but it cannot explain
> what you are doing or talk you through it. Follow along with a few guided videos first - see
> [Going Further](#going-further) for where to find good ones. Come back to these presets once
> you know how the rhythms are meant to feel; the watch is then the better tool for keeping
> them.

### Included Sessions

| Session      | Rhythm                             | Length |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------ |
| Box Breath   | 4-4-4-4                            | 5:04   |
| B. Coherence | 6-6                                | 5:00   |
| B. 4-7-8     | 4-7-8                              | 5:04   |
| B. Energize  | fast breathing, then box breathing | 5:20   |
| B. Wind Down | long exhales, then 4-7-8           | 4:54   |
| B. Holds     | deep breaths, then breath holds    | 7:16   |
| B. Sleep     | slowing down, then 10 min resting  | 23:08  |
| B. Calm      | ever longer exhales, no holds      | 5:00   |
| B. Free      | breathe at your own pace           | 15:00  |

**Box Breathing**  
Equal inhale, hold, exhale, hold.  
Often used to calm the nervous system.

**Coherence Breathing**  
Equal, slow inhale and exhale.  
Often associated with increased HRV.

**4-7-8 Breathing**  
Inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8.  
Commonly used before sleep.

**Sleep**  
Starts at an easy pace and slows your breath step by step to about two breaths a minute, eases
back, then leaves you 10 minutes to breathe freely. It ends with a single soft blip. To let it
finish and save on its own if you fall asleep, keep **Auto Stop** on and set **Confirm Save** to
**Auto Yes & Exit**.

**Calm**  
Longer and longer exhales, with no breath holds at all.  
For stress and anxious moments, any time of day.

**Free**  
No rhythm to follow: 15 minutes of breathing at your own pace while the watch records heart
rate, HRV, stress and respiration.

You can edit any of these, or build your own - see [Guided Breathwork](#custom-breathwork-patterns).

### What Happens During a Session

Every phase change has its own vibration:

- **rising** - breathe in
- **a short blip** - hold
- **falling** - breathe out

That alone is enough to follow a whole session with your eyes closed.

If you do look at the watch, it shows:

- the current phase (**Inhale**, **Hold**, **Exhale**) and a countdown to the next change
- a ring that fills as the phase progresses
- the breathing route, when the session specifies nose or mouth

Press **up** or **down** (or swipe) to move between four screens:

- **Guidance** - the phase, countdown and route above
- **Breath ball** - a ball that grows as you breathe in, shrinks as you breathe out and stays
  still while you hold
- **Zen** - a dark screen with only the session ring
- **Metrics** - your heart rate, HRV, stress and respiration

The vibrations continue on every screen. Your next breathwork session opens on the screen you
ended this one on.

Before the session starts, the preparation countdown shows what is coming.

### Gentle Tips

- Inhale through the nose if possible
- Longer exhales than inhales often help calm
- The session tells you when to use the mouth - 4-7-8, for example, exhales through the mouth
- If timed breathing feels stressful, choose a simple session and breathe naturally
- Never practise breath holds in water, while driving, or while standing up
- For guided sessions with a voice and music, see [Going Further](#going-further)

<a id="understanding-your-session-data"></a>

## Understanding Your Session Data

### HRV (Heart Rate Variability)

HRV reflects how much the time between heartbeats varies.

- Higher HRV → calmer, more regulated states
- Lower HRV → activation or stress

Variability is **healthy** - it is not something to fix.

### Stress

Lower stress values feel pleasant, but higher stress does **not** mean a bad session.

Sometimes a session is about staying present with discomfort.

### Heart Rate

Heart rate often slows as the body settles and usually aligns with HRV and stress.

### Respiration

Respiration data comes from Garmin sensors and is best used for trends, not precision.

From the session picker:

- Hold the **menu button** (or touch and hold the screen)
- Open **Add New** or **Edit**

You can configure:

### Session Basics

- Duration (H:MM)
- Color (used in visual indicators)
- Vibration pattern
- Activity type (Meditation / Yoga / Breathwork)
- HRV tracking level
- Custom session name (supports `[time]` placeholder)

### Interval Alerts

- One-off or repeating alerts
- Durations from seconds to hours
- Offset (delay before first trigger)
- Color per alert (or transparent to hide)
- Vibration or sound pattern

Only one alert runs at a time.  
Priority order: final alert → last one-off → last repeating alert.

<a id="custom-breathwork-patterns"></a>

## Guided Breathwork

Every guided breathwork session is built from a **Breath Program**: an ordered list of steps.
The [included sessions](#breathwork-sessions) are ordinary programs, so you can open any of
them and change it. To add guidance to another session, use its **Breath Program** row.

Adding a step always starts from a ready-made pattern (`4-4-4-4`, `4-7-8`, `6-6`, `4-4-4`,
`4-8`, `2-2`, a hold, a rest, or a custom one), so you rarely have to enter four numbers by hand.

### Steps

Each step is a breath pattern plus a repeat rule:

| Field           | Meaning                                        |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Inhale          | seconds breathing in (`0:00` skips this phase) |
| Hold            | seconds holding with full lungs                |
| Exhale          | seconds breathing out                          |
| Hold empty      | seconds holding with empty lungs               |
| Breathe in via  | nose, mouth, or not set                        |
| Breathe out via | nose, mouth, or not set                        |
| Repeat          | either a number of rounds, or a duration       |

A pure breath-hold is just a step where only a hold is set. Steps are named automatically
from their numbers, so `4-7-8` means inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8.

A **Rest** step has no pattern, only a duration: the app stops pacing you, shows
_Breathe freely_ with a countdown, and keeps recording. Use it to breathe normally after a
hold or a fast-breathing step, to settle for a minute before the first pattern, or as a quiet
rest at the end of the session - put `Rest 10:00` last and the session runs ten more minutes
before it stops.

Example - `4 rounds of 5-5-5, then a 2:00 hold, a 1:00 rest, then 6-6 for 3 minutes`:

| Step | Pattern   | Repeat    | Length   |
| ---- | --------- | --------- | -------- |
| 1    | 5-5-5     | 4 rounds  | 1:00     |
| 2    | Hold 2:00 | once      | 2:00     |
| 3    | Rest      | for 1:00  | 1:00     |
| 4    | 6-6       | for 3:00  | 3:00     |
|      |           | **total** | **7:00** |

**The program sets the session length.** With a program in place the Time row shows the
computed total and opens the program editor instead of a duration picker.

Use **Move up** / **Move down** inside a step to reorder.

The breathing route is set **per step**, so a program can switch between nose and mouth as it
goes - for example fast breathing out through the mouth first, then slow nasal breathing to settle.

Limits: 10 steps per program, 99 rounds per step, 9:59 per phase, 59:59 per rest.

### Reading the Guidance Screen

Beyond the phase word and countdown, the screen carries two rings:

- the **inner ring** fills over the current phase and is coloured by phase (during a rest it
  fills over the whole rest)
- the **outer ring** is overall session progress, with tick marks where the steps change. The
  same ticks show on every session screen and on the timeline bar of the session picker. Breath
  holds are marked orange and **Breathe freely** steps grey, so you can see them coming

The line under the countdown shows your round within the current step (`3/4`) and, when the
step specifies one, the breathing route for the phase you are in.

### The Breath Ball

One screen down from the guidance screen. The ball grows through each inhale and shrinks through
each exhale - blue in, green out - slow at the start and end of every breath, like a real one.
During a hold it stays still in the colour of the breath before it - blue on full lungs, green on
empty - and its outline counts the hold down second by second. A rest shows a mid-size grey ball
and **Breathe freely**. The phase word sits in the middle; the outer
ring and its step ticks are the same as on the guidance screen.

### Cues

Each phase change fires its own vibration, so a session works with your eyes closed and the
screen off:

| Phase                | Vibration  | Tone              |
| -------------------- | ---------- | ----------------- |
| Inhale               | ascending  | rising two notes  |
| Hold (full or empty) | short blip | one short note    |
| Exhale               | descending | falling two notes |
| Rest                 | short blip | one short note    |

Both holds share the same cue. A hold always follows an inhale or an exhale, so you already
know whether your lungs are full or empty without a fourth pattern to learn. A rest uses the
same neutral cue: it marks that the pacing has stopped, and the next step opens with its own
inhale cue.

**Settings → Breath Cues** switches these between Off, Vibration, and Vibration + Tone.
Tones only play on watches that support them. They also obey the global Vibration & Sound
setting. Cues keep firing while you are on the metrics screen, so you can look at your heart
rate without losing the rhythm.

### Before the Session Starts

The preparation countdown shows the program summary, so you know what is coming before the
first inhale. When every step uses the same breathing route it is shown here too; when the
steps differ, watch the guidance screen instead.

Programs longer than four steps are summarised as `+N more`.

> Interval alerts still work exactly as before and can be combined with a breath program -
> they are best used for periodic reminders rather than for breathing patterns.

## Advanced HRV Metrics

When HRV Tracking is set to **On Detailed**, the app records:

- RMSSD
- Successive heartbeat differences
- pNN20
- pNN50
- Raw heartbeat intervals
- HR derived from heartbeat intervals
- RMSSD rolling window
- SDRR (first 5 minutes)
- SDRR (last 5 minutes)

These values are for **exploration and learning**, not evaluation.

## Global Settings (Defaults for All Sessions)

Access via:

- Session picker → hold menu → Global Settings
- Hold menu again to edit

### HRV Tracking (Default)

- Off
- On
- On Detailed (default)

### Confirm Save

- Ask
- Auto Yes
- Auto No

### Multi-Session Mode

- Yes - app stays open for multiple sessions
- No - app exits after each session
- With Learn Routine on, once you have a regular sequence (e.g. breathwork, then silent sitting),
  "Next session" shows the name of the session you usually do next and opens it, so the sequence
  runs without scrolling. Sequences are also learned without Multi-Session Mode, when you reopen
  the app within an hour after a session.

### Preparation Time

- 0 / 15 / 30 / 60 seconds

### Respiration Rate

- On / Off (device dependent)

### Default Activity Type

- Meditation
- Yoga
- Breathwork

### HRV Window

- Short windows show fast changes
- 3-5 minutes recommended for reliability  
  (Default: 60 seconds)

### Vibration & Sound

- Enable or disable all vibration and sound alerts

### Use Session Name as Activity Name

- Uses custom session name in Garmin Connect
- Supports `[time]` placeholder
- Activity names limited to 21 characters

### Learn Routine

- On (default) - the session picker opens on the session you usually do at this time, or after a
  session on the one that usually comes next, and Multi-Session Mode offers that one too
- Off - the picker always opens on the last selected session; what was learned is forgotten the
  next time you open the app

### Sensor Restart

- Turns off all HR sensors and exits the app, the same as closing it
- If HRV gets stuck, see [HRV does not start](#faqs-troubleshooting) in the FAQ

# FAQs & Troubleshooting

<a id="faq-hrv-sensor-data"></a>

## HRV & Sensor Data

<a id="faq-hrv"></a>

<details>
  <summary><strong>HRV does not start / keeps showing “Restart the app”</strong></summary>

  <p>Try the following steps in order:</p>

  <ol>
    <li><strong>Close and reopen the app.</strong> This usually works within seconds; if not, repeat it once or twice.</li>
    <li><strong>Wear the watch a little tighter</strong>, about a finger's width above the wrist bone, then reopen the app and hold still for a moment.</li>
    <li><strong>Disconnect external sensors you are not using.</strong> In your watch's connectivity settings (not the app settings), make sure no chest strap or other heart-rate sensor is connected. A nearby chest strap sometimes reconnects automatically and disrupts readings.</li>
    <li><strong>Reinstall the app</strong> if the issue persists, especially if it got worse after an app or watch update.</li>
    <li><strong>Use a chest strap</strong> or another external heart-rate sensor that supports heartbeat intervals if you still do not get reliable HRV. It is supported automatically, no app setting required.</li>
  </ol>

  <p><strong>Tip:</strong> if you do not need HRV for this session, just start it. Your heart rate is recorded either way.</p>

  <p><strong>Why this happens:</strong> it is almost always your watch's heart-rate sensor, not the app. Your watch reports individual heartbeats (needed for HRV) separately from your regular heart rate, so heart rate showing up does not mean HRV is ready. After sitting unused, for example first thing in the morning, the sensor sometimes does not deliver heartbeats at all until the app is reopened, and waiting longer does not help. The app cannot fix this from the inside.</p>

  <p>On some watches it regularly takes a restart or two. This is normal and does not harm anything. How quickly HRV starts can also change after a watch firmware update or a reinstall, for better or worse, even when the app itself has not changed.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>The respiration rate (breaths per minute) is off</strong></summary>

  <p>What helps most:</p>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Wear the watch snugly</strong> and keep your wrist still.</li>
    <li><strong>Use a chest strap</strong> that supports HRV and respiration reporting (for example HRM-600) if accurate respiration data is important to you.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Why this happens:</strong> the app uses the respiration rate Garmin provides. Garmin derives it from the rhythm of your heartbeats - the time between beats shortens a little as you inhale and lengthens as you exhale - so it needs a clean heartbeat signal, and it can be less reliable during very calm, still practices.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>The app does not track stress (but the old version did)</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Your watch model may only support stress snapshots.</strong> Some older models (for example the Fenix 5X) only take stress snapshots, not real-time stress during activities, so stress data may be missing or limited. This depends on the watch, not the app.</p>

  <p><strong>Why the old version showed stress:</strong> it calculated its own stress estimate. The app now uses Garmin's built-in stress tracking instead, so its values match Garmin Connect - two different stress values for the same session confused many users.</p>

</details>

<a id="faq-during-a-session"></a>

## During a Session

<a id="faq-screen-bright"></a>

<details>
  <summary><strong>The screen is too bright, especially in the dark during a session</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Lower the brightness in your watch's Focus Mode for activities.</strong> The app does not control it: by default, Garmin activates a focus mode for activities that may set screen brightness to around 80%.</p>

  <ol>
    <li>Open Watch Settings (on touch watches such as Venu or vívoactive: swipe left from the watch face and select Settings)</li>
    <li>Go to Focus Modes → Activity</li>
    <li>Under Display &amp; Brightness, <strong>lower the Brightness</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Turn off Always On Display</strong> - the screen then switches off after the Timeout set there and comes back with a wrist gesture or a button press</li>
  </ol>

  <p>This affects all activities. <strong>Advanced option:</strong> create a custom Focus Mode that triggers only for Meditation, Yoga, and Breathwork activities.</p>

  <p><strong>Tip:</strong> for an even darker screen, press <strong>UP</strong> or <strong>DOWN</strong> (or swipe) during a session to open the zen screen. It is black except for a thin progress ring, and the time fades out after a few seconds. On AMOLED watches black pixels are switched off, so it gives off almost no light. Your next session opens on the zen screen again.</p>

</details>

<a id="faq-sounds"></a>

<details>
  <summary><strong>How do I turn the sounds off and keep only the vibration?</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>There are two sound sources, turned off in different places:</strong> the watch's activity beeps (in your watch settings) and this app's alerts (in the session settings).</p>

  <p><strong>1. The beeps when a session starts, stops, or is saved</strong> are Garmin's built-in activity tones. The app cannot silence them, which is why you still hear them with Global Settings → Vibration &amp; Sound set to Off.</p>

  <p><em>On newer watches with Focus Modes</em> (fēnix 8, fēnix E, tactix 8, Enduro 3, Forerunner 570/970, Venu 3/4, vívoactive 5/6):</p>

  <ol>
    <li>From the watch face, hold the <strong>MENU</strong> button</li>
    <li>Select Watch Settings → Focus Modes → Activity → Sound &amp; Vibe</li>
    <li><strong>Turn on Silent Mode</strong> (if there is none, set Alert Tones and Button Tones to Off)</li>
    <li><strong>Leave Vibration on</strong> - it is a separate setting</li>
  </ol>

  <p>The activity focus mode overrides your general sound settings during an activity, so silencing tones only under Watch Settings → Sound &amp; Vibe often does not help. If you still hear beeps, change both.</p>

  <p><strong>Advanced option:</strong> to silence only your sessions, add a focus mode (Watch Settings → Focus Modes → Add → Activity) with Silent Mode on and set Auto-Triggered to Meditation, Yoga and Breathwork.</p>

  <p><em>On older watches without Focus Modes</em> (fēnix 6/7, epix Gen 2, Forerunner 245/255/265/955/965, Venu 2):</p>

  <ol>
    <li>From the watch face, hold <strong>MENU</strong> (or <strong>UP</strong>)</li>
    <li>Select Settings → System → Sound and Vibration (on some models: Sounds)</li>
    <li><strong>Set Alert Tones to Off</strong> and leave Vibration on</li>
  </ol>

  <p><strong>2. The alerts from this app</strong> (session end and interval alerts): open Add New or Edit for a session, select Vibe / Sound and <strong>choose any pattern except Long Sound and Short Sound</strong>. Those two play a tone and do not vibrate; every other pattern only vibrates. Interval alerts have the same choice under Interval Alerts → Edit → Vibe / Sound.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>How do I run an open-ended session?</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Turn off Auto Stop in Global Settings</strong>, so sessions continue until you stop them. Or create a very long session (for example 9 hours) and stop it when you are finished.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Can I listen to music while using the app?</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Not from the app, but you can play music alongside it.</strong> Start music separately (for example the built-in music player or Spotify), then run your session. To reach the music controls (varies by model):</p>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Hot key:</strong> assign Music controls to a hot key (often under Settings → System → Hot Keys / Controls).</li>
    <li><strong>Controls menu:</strong> open the watch's controls menu and select Music.</li>
    <li><strong>Touchscreen shortcut</strong> (some models): set the shortcut control to music controls (<a href="https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/venu/EN-US/GUID-5E9D3D0F-D4C9-4A4A-97D6-4F178C27DAD8.html">example in the Venu manual</a>).</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Interval alerts can be timed to your music, but there is no beat-sync, so they will not stay aligned with a track.</p>

</details>

<a id="faq-sessions"></a>

## Sessions

<details>
  <summary><strong>The app crashes the moment I start a session - what can I do?</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Choose a different activity type:</strong> edit the session and set its activity type to Meditation, Yoga or Breathwork. If your device offers a more generic activity type, that usually works on all models.</p>

  <p><strong>Why this happens:</strong> your watch model likely does not support the selected activity type.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Can I change the name of the session on the watch?</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Yes, on the watch:</strong> open Add New or Edit for a session, select Name and enter the text with the watch's text picker.</p>

  <p>To also use it as the activity name in Garmin Connect, turn on Global Settings → Session name = Activity name. The placeholder <code>[time]</code> adds the session length: <code>Deep rest [time]</code> → <code>Deep rest 20min</code>.</p>

</details>

## Watch & Installation

<a id="faq-install"></a>

<details>
  <summary><strong>I installed the app, but it does not show up on my watch (or installation is stuck)</strong></summary>

  <p>What you can try (these also help if the app is stuck on “Installing…” or only partially installed):</p>

  <ol>
    <li><strong>Look in your watch's Activities / Apps list</strong>, where you start a run, yoga or breathwork. The app may not show up in widget, glance or shortcut lists.</li>
    <li><strong>Sync your watch again</strong> in Garmin Connect.</li>
    <li><strong><a href="#restart-watch">Restart the watch</a> and your phone</strong>, then sync again. If the watch seems frozen during installation, restart it first.</li>
    <li><strong>Uninstall the app, restart the watch, then reinstall</strong> the app and sync again.</li>
    <li>
      <strong>Contact Garmin Support</strong> if it still does not appear:
      <a href="https://support.garmin.com/en-US/?faq=67C7Y58Nxb6rkxt2Enbrf5">Connect IQ install troubleshooting</a> or the <a href="https://support.garmin.com/">main support portal</a>.
    </li>
  </ol>

  <p><strong>Why this happens:</strong> installing and syncing Connect IQ apps is handled entirely by Garmin (Garmin Connect / Connect IQ / Garmin Express). The app cannot influence whether it appears on your watch, and there is nothing I can change in the code to fix an installation or sync issue.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Parts of the app are shown in the wrong language</strong></summary>

  <p>If a few texts (for example HRV labels) show up in a different language while the rest of the app is correct, try these in order:</p>

  <ol>
    <li><strong>Uninstall the app</strong> from your watch</li>
    <li><strong><a href="#restart-watch">Restart (reboot) your watch</a></strong></li>
    <li><strong>Reinstall the app</strong> and sync again</li>
  </ol>

  <p>If it still happens, please open the Contact Developer link in the Connect IQ Store and include your watch model, watch firmware version, and which language you see incorrectly.</p>

  <p><strong>Why this happens:</strong> it is usually a one-off installation or sync hiccup.</p>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>GPS turns on during my session, my location is added to the activity name, or the battery drains afterwards (Venu 2 / Venu 2S / Venu Sq 2)</strong></summary>

  <p>Until Garmin fixes it:</p>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Restart your watch after your session</strong> if the battery drains (see <a href="#restart-watch">How do I restart (reboot) my Garmin watch?</a>).</li>
    <li><strong>Comment on the Garmin bug report</strong> linked below - more reports help Garmin prioritize the fix.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Why this happens:</strong> a firmware bug on these three watches (not reported on any other model) switches GPS on for every activity type the app offers (Meditation, Breathwork, Yoga, Generic). The activity then gets your location in its name (for example <code>&lt;My City&gt; Breathwork</code>) and a map in Garmin Connect, and GPS may stay on after you close the app, which drains the battery until the watch is restarted. The app has no GPS permission at all, so it can neither cause nor fix this. The issue has been reported to Garmin: <a href="https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/i/bug-reports/app-activates-tracks-gps-despite-missing-permissions">Connect IQ bug report</a>.</p>

</details>

<details id="restart-watch">
  <summary><strong>How do I restart (reboot) my Garmin watch?</strong></summary>

  <p><strong>Hold the LIGHT / power button until the watch turns off</strong> (often ~10-15 seconds, longer if it is frozen), wait a few seconds, then press it again. Or open Settings → System → Power Off or Restart (wording varies by model).</p>

  <p>A restart does not erase your data.</p>

</details>
